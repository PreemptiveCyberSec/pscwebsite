Ransomware Readiness Assessment

PSC assesses ransomware readiness across the full path from initial access and privilege escalation to defense impairment, data theft, recovery disruption, encryption, and extortion. The review connects preventive controls with detection, containment, backup integrity, restoration evidence, and decision ownership.

This service is for organizations that need a practical view of whether they can interrupt and recover from a ransomware scenario. The outcome is an evidence-based scorecard and prioritized resilience roadmap, not a guarantee that one product or backup copy prevents every incident.

Subject-matter owner: Mark Chen · Security ResearcherReviewer: Viral Maniar · Managing Director and Principal Consultant
Risk focus

Problems this engagement addresses

The scope follows credible attack and failure paths rather than a generic control checklist.

  • Common entry paths remain exposed through identity, remote access, email, applications, or unmanaged assets.
  • Endpoint and identity controls do not reliably interrupt privilege escalation and lateral movement.
  • Defense impairment, recovery changes, staging, and precursor behavior are not detected early enough.
  • Backups are reachable from production identities or have not been restored under realistic conditions.
  • Containment, communications, legal, executive, and recovery decisions lack tested ownership.
Engagement fit

When this service is useful

A clear fit decision protects assessment quality and prevents a broad request from becoming a shallow exercise.

Designed for

  • Organizations preparing for an exercise, insurance review, major change, or resilience investment decision.
  • Security and infrastructure teams that can provide control, identity, network, backup, and recovery evidence.
  • Leadership seeking a cross-functional roadmap rather than a single-tool assessment.
  • Teams that need to test assumptions about containment and restoration before an incident.

Not the right fit for

  • An active ransomware incident requiring immediate containment and incident response.
  • A backup product configuration review with no wider identity, detection, or recovery context.
  • A certification, insurance guarantee, or promise that extortion cannot occur.
Scope

Choose the boundary that matches the decision

Options can be combined after owners, environments, authority, safety limits, and expected evidence are agreed.

Control readiness

Review exposure, identity, hardening, endpoint, email, segmentation, detection, and response controls against common ransomware behaviors.

Backup and recovery

Examine isolation, immutability, administrative paths, monitoring, restoration procedures, dependencies, and available test evidence.

Scenario validation

Walk through or safely simulate a representative precursor and impact scenario to test evidence, decisions, containment, and recovery coordination.

Prerequisites

  • Named owners for security, identity, infrastructure, backup, recovery, incident management, and executive decisions.
  • Current architecture, critical services, identity paths, backup design, and incident procedures.
  • Representative control evidence, alert examples, restoration records, and prior exercise or incident lessons.
  • Agreement on whether validation is document-based, workshop-led, or includes controlled technical tests.

Explicit exclusions

  • Live destructive encryption, recovery deletion, uncontrolled malware, or denial of service.
  • Incident response retainers or emergency response unless separately contracted.
  • Formal legal, regulatory, insurance, or business-continuity certification.
  • Recovery guarantees for systems or dependencies that cannot be safely tested.
Methodology

Evidence at every phase

Each phase produces an observable input to the final risk and remediation decisions.

  1. Critical-service and scenario definition

    Identify business-critical services, credible entry paths, identity and infrastructure dependencies, and a representative ransomware scenario.

  2. Preventive control review

    Assess attack surface, email and remote access, identity tiers, privilege, endpoint hardening, segmentation, and administrative boundaries.

  3. Detection and containment assessment

    Map precursor behaviors to telemetry, alerts, investigation, isolation, credential control, and network containment decisions.

  4. Backup and recovery validation

    Review immutability, isolation, administrative access, monitoring, restoration evidence, dependency order, and clean-recovery assumptions.

  5. Roadmap and exercise

    Prioritize gaps across prevent, detect, contain, recover, and govern, then validate selected decisions or controls through an agreed scenario.

Outputs

Deliverables built for decisions and fixes

Reporting separates observed evidence, uncertainty, business priority, and the action needed to verify remediation.

  • Ransomware readiness scorecard with evidence and confidence notes.
  • Attack-path and critical-service dependency view.
  • Detection, containment, backup, and restoration gap analysis.
  • Prioritized resilience roadmap with accountable control areas.
  • Executive workshop and optional focused validation plan.

Severity approach

Gaps are prioritized by credible attack-path contribution, critical-service impact, privilege and blast radius, detection opportunity, backup exposure, restoration uncertainty, and the time sensitivity of containment decisions.

Readout

The executive readout explains the scenario, critical assumptions, likely decision points, and highest-value resilience investments. Technical owners receive evidence, dependencies, and validation actions for each gap.

Remediation support

PSC supports control owners in refining identity tiers, segmentation, telemetry, containment procedures, backup administration, restore testing, and cross-functional playbooks. Recommendations identify dependencies that one team cannot fix alone.

Retest policy

Retesting targets the original evidence gap: a corrected identity path, a new detection, an isolation workflow, or a restoration test. Destructive behavior is simulated or exercised through safe artifacts and agreed test systems.

FAQ

Questions buyers ask before scoping

Final rules, access, and deliverables are confirmed in the engagement scope.

Is this a backup assessment?

Backup and restoration are essential parts, but readiness also depends on entry paths, identity, privilege, segmentation, detection, containment, crisis decisions, and critical-service dependencies.

Will PSC run ransomware in our environment?

No uncontrolled malware or destructive encryption is required. Technical validation uses safe behaviors, simulations, configuration evidence, or agreed test systems to examine the same control and decision points.

Can the assessment support a tabletop exercise?

Yes. The evidence and scenario can inform a cross-functional exercise, or a workshop can be included to test escalation, containment, communications, and recovery decisions.

What does recovery validation include?

It examines isolation and administrative paths, immutability assumptions, monitoring, restoration records, dependency order, clean-system decisions, and whether representative services have been restored successfully.
