Detection Engineering

PSC detection engineering turns priority threats into observable behaviors, required data, tested analytics, and analyst decisions. The work connects ATT&CK techniques to the telemetry your environment actually produces, then validates whether detections are reliable, explainable, and actionable.

This service is for SOC, SIEM, and security-platform teams that need to build a detection program, reduce noisy legacy rules, or close known coverage gaps. The outcome is a governed detection catalogue with test evidence, tuning decisions, and practical triage guidance.

Subject-matter owner: Mark Chen · Security ResearcherReviewer: Viral Maniar · Managing Director and Principal Consultant
Risk focus

Problems this engagement addresses

The scope follows credible attack and failure paths rather than a generic control checklist.

  • Large rule inventories with unknown ownership, quality, or connection to priority threats.
  • Telemetry gaps that make important attacker behaviors impossible to observe or reconstruct.
  • High false-positive volume caused by missing context, weak baselines, or brittle indicators.
  • Detections deployed without repeatable tests, versioning, or expected analyst outcomes.
  • ATT&CK coverage measured by rule count rather than validated behavioral visibility.
Engagement fit

When this service is useful

A clear fit decision protects assessment quality and prevents a broad request from becoming a shallow exercise.

Designed for

  • SOC and SIEM teams building or rationalizing a detection catalogue.
  • Organizations onboarding new endpoint, identity, cloud, network, or application telemetry.
  • Purple-team or incident lessons that need to become durable analytics and playbooks.
  • Security leaders who need an evidence-based view of detection coverage and gaps.

Not the right fit for

  • A request to install a SIEM without threat priorities, data ownership, or operating processes.
  • A promise of zero false positives or complete ATT&CK coverage.
  • Twenty-four-hour managed monitoring unless separately scoped as an operating service.

Prerequisites

  • Named threat priorities, critical assets, and security operations stakeholders.
  • Access to representative schemas, sample events, retention details, and existing rules.
  • A test path for analytics and controlled validation before production enforcement.
  • Owners for telemetry onboarding, SIEM content, analyst playbooks, and change approval.

Explicit exclusions

  • Licensing, procurement, or operation of third-party SIEM and EDR products.
  • Data-source onboarding that requires unapproved production changes.
  • Incident response or continuous alert handling outside the agreed engineering scope.
  • Coverage claims for unavailable or unvalidated telemetry.
Methodology

Evidence at every phase

Each phase produces an observable input to the final risk and remediation decisions.

  1. Threat and coverage model

    Prioritize adversary behaviors, critical workflows, assets, and ATT&CK techniques based on business and incident context.

  2. Telemetry assessment

    Map each behavior to available events, required fields, provenance, latency, retention, joins, and known blind spots.

  3. Detection design

    Write behavioral logic, context enrichment, thresholds, suppression, expected evidence, and analyst outcomes.

  4. Validation and tuning

    Exercise representative behaviors, test historical data, measure false positives, and document limitations and safe exceptions.

  5. Operational handover

    Version detections, define ownership and review cadence, deliver triage playbooks, and prioritize remaining data or coverage gaps.

Outputs

Deliverables built for decisions and fixes

Reporting separates observed evidence, uncertainty, business priority, and the action needed to verify remediation.

  • Threat-prioritized detection use-case catalogue.
  • Telemetry and field requirement matrix with identified gaps.
  • Versioned detection logic and validation evidence.
  • Triage, enrichment, escalation, and response playbooks.
  • Coverage dashboard design and continuous-review backlog.

Severity approach

Detection work is prioritized by threat relevance, asset criticality, behavior impact, telemetry confidence, existing prevention, expected alert volume, and the cost of a miss. ATT&CK technique counts are supporting context, not a severity score.

Readout

PSC reviews the coverage model with leadership and walks analysts and engineers through logic, evidence, tuning, blind spots, and expected decisions. Limitations remain visible so coverage is not overstated.

Remediation support

Remediation can include telemetry corrections, field normalization, context joins, rule redesign, baseline changes, suppression governance, and playbook updates. Product-specific implementation is agreed against the team's supported platforms.

Retest policy

Detections are re-executed against controlled behavior or approved historical datasets after changes. Validation records expected events, alert output, enrichment, analyst path, and known environmental exceptions.

FAQ

Questions buyers ask before scoping

Final rules, access, and deliverables are confirmed in the engagement scope.

Can PSC write rules for our SIEM or EDR?

Yes, when the platform, schemas, test path, and deployment process are in scope. The service starts with behavior and evidence requirements so product syntax does not become the only definition of coverage.

How do you reduce false positives?

PSC tests logic against representative data, adds identity and asset context, documents baselines and exceptions, and tunes thresholds with analysts. Noise is measured against the risk of suppressing meaningful behavior.

Does ATT&CK coverage prove we can detect an attack?

No. A mapping shows intent, not validated visibility. PSC records the required telemetry, test evidence, logic limitations, and analyst outcome for each priority use case.

Can this follow a red or purple team exercise?

Yes. Exercise evidence provides concrete behaviors, timestamps, and missed opportunities that can be converted into tested analytics and playbook improvements.
